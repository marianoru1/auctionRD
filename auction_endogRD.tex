A One-Parameter Model of Overbidding in Private and Common Value Settings}}

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Laboratory subjects overbid relative to the risk-neutral Bayesian--Nash benchmark
in private- and common-value auctions. Expectations-based reference dependence of
the K\H{o}szegi--Rabin type is often proposed as an explanation, but its canonical
sealed-bid formulation separates a \emph{good} dimension from a \emph{money}
dimension. In induced-value experiments the object's worth is itself a monetary
redemption value, so the two dimensions collapse. We therefore study
K\H{o}szegi--Rabin preferences with a \emph{single monetary dimension} and a
self-consistent reference point, and add one reference-independent
parameter---a joy-of-winning bonus $w\ge0$ received upon winning. The model nests
pure loss aversion at $w=0$ and yields closed-form equilibria. In the private-value
first-price auction, loss aversion alone already generates overbidding for every
valuation (a first-order risk-aversion channel), at a rate increasing in the
loss-aversion index $\Lambda$; the joy-of-winning bonus amplifies it and produces a
strictly positive bid at a zero valuation, $w/(1-\Lambda)$. In the first-price
wallet game, loss aversion alone has \emph{no} effect on the equilibrium bid
($\beta^*(x)=x$ at $w=0$), while any $w>0$ yields overbidding
$\beta^*(x)-x=w/(1-\Lambda+\Lambda x)$. The contrast is sharp and testable: the
presence of common-value overbidding \emph{beyond} the winner's curse identifies a
joy-of-winning motive, whereas private-value overbidding is consistent with pure
loss aversion. The framework provides a tractable baseline for structural
estimation and richer behavioral auction models.
\end{abstract}




\section{DeepSeek 4.1 Flash - Introduction}
A robust experimental regularity is that bidders bid above the risk-neutral
Bayesian--Nash benchmark across a wide range of auction environments: first- and
second-price private-value auctions \citep{cox1982,coxSmithWalker1988,goeree2002,
crawfordIriberri2007}, and first- and second-price common-value auctions
\citep{kagelLevin1986,eysterRabin2005,crawfordIriberri2007}. Explanations include
risk aversion \citep{cox1982,coxSmithWalker1988}, anticipated regret
\citep{engelbrechtWiggans1989,filizOzbay2007}, quantal response and level-$k$
reasoning \citep{goeree2002,crawfordIriberri2007}, cursedness \citep{eysterRabin2005},
and a non-monetary utility of winning \citep{delgado2008}.
Expectations-based reference dependence \citep{koszegiRabin2006,koszegiRabin2007},
as applied to sealed-bid auctions by \citet{langeRatan2010}, is a leading candidate.
Its canonical auction formulation, however, is \emph{multi-dimensional}: the good
and money are treated as separate consumption dimensions, which is what generates
the ``attachment'' to the object and the resulting high-value overbidding. This is a
substantial assumption in induced-value laboratory auctions, where the object being
sold is a token whose entire value is a monetary redemption value. There, the good
and money dimensions are economically the same, and it is arguably not legitimate to
evaluate gain--loss separately on each.
This paper takes the induced-value setting seriously and restricts attention to a
\emph{single monetary dimension}, with preferences defined over the net monetary
payoff and a self-consistent (personal-equilibrium) reference point. In a single
dimension, K\H{o}szegi--Rabin preferences reduce to \emph{first-order risk aversion}
\citep{koszegiRabin2007}: the kink in the gain--loss function makes agents averse to
mean-preserving spreads in a way that ordinary expected-utility risk aversion is
not. This channel has sharply different implications from the two-dimensional
model, so the choice of dimensionality is not innocuous.
To this baseline we add a single reference-independent parameter: a direct utility
bonus $w\ge0$ obtained upon winning, the ``joy of winning'' \citep{delgado2008}.
Because the bonus is direct rather than a change in the good's intrinsic value, it
is not itself subject to gain--loss comparison and is well defined whether or not
the good is a separate dimension. The model nests pure loss aversion at $w=0$ and
admits closed-form equilibria in the two canonical first-price settings.
Our results are as follows. In the private-value first-price auction, loss aversion
alone generates overbidding for \emph{every} valuation; the joy-of-winning bonus
amplifies the overbidding and yields a positive bid at a zero valuation, which pure
loss aversion cannot. In the first-price wallet game of \citet{klemperer1998}, loss
aversion alone has \emph{no} effect: the equilibrium coincides with the risk-neutral
benchmark $\beta_0(x)=x$. Any strictly positive joy-of-winning bonus, however,
produces uniform overbidding. This delivers a clean, testable asymmetry:
\emph{private-value} overbidding is consistent with pure loss aversion, while
\emph{common-value} overbidding beyond the winner's curse requires a
joy-of-winning motive. Together with the format-invariant intercept $w/(1-\Lambda)$,
this provides a small set of moments to separate the two motives.
Section~\ref{sec:framework} presents the framework and discusses dimensionality.
Section~\ref{sec:pv} and Section~\ref{sec:wallet} analyze the private-value auction
and the wallet game in the first-price format; Section~\ref{sec:spa} analyzes the two
second-price environments. Section~\ref{sec:id} discusses identification and revenue.
Section~\ref{sec:ext} sketches extensions. An appendix states the two-dimensional
results for comparison.
\section{Framework}
\label{sec:framework}
\subsection{Preferences over a single monetary dimension}
Consumption is the net monetary payoff $\pi$. Following \citet{koszegiRabin2006},
utility relative to reference payoff $r$ is
\begin{equation}
u(\pi\mid r)=\pi+\mu(\pi-r),
\end{equation}
where $\mu$ is piecewise linear,
\begin{equation}
\mu(x)=\begin{cases}\eta\,x & x\ge0,\\ \eta\lambda\,x & x<0,\end{cases}
\qquad \lambda>1,\ \eta>0 .
\end{equation}
We set $\eta=1$ and write $\Lambda\equiv\eta(\lambda-1)>0$. The reference point is the
distribution of consumption induced by the agent's own planned action (a personal
equilibrium), and utility is the expectation of $u(\pi\mid r)$ over an independent
draw $r$ from that distribution. Because $\mu$ is kinked at zero, reference
dependence over a single dimension induces first-order risk aversion
\citep{koszegiRabin2007}, which behaves like risk aversion in first-price auctions
but is a distinct, reference-dependent primitive.
\subsection{Dimensionality and induced values}
\label{sec:dim}
The standard auction application of these preferences
\citep{langeRatan2010} separates the good ($k=1$) and money ($k=2$):
$u(c\mid r)=\sum_k[m_k(c_k)+\mu(m_k(c_k)-m_k(r_k))]$. The good dimension contributes
gain--loss when the bidder does not obtain the object, generating attachment. This
requires $m_1$ to be a consumption dimension psychologically distinct from money.
In an induced-value auction the object's value is a cash redemption value, so
$m_1(v)=v$ is money and the two dimensions coincide. We therefore use the
single-dimension formulation above throughout. A brief two-dimensional comparison is
provided in Appendix~\ref{app:two}. Which dimensionality applies is ultimately an
empirical question; the two models make distinct, testable predictions (see
Section~\ref{sec:id}).
\subsection{Joy of winning}
A bidder who wins receives a direct utility bonus $w\ge0$, modeled additively as
$w\cdot P$, where $P$ is the probability of winning. Being direct and
reference-independent, this specification is valid under either dimensionality.
\begin{remark}[Alternative specification]
If instead $w$ entered the intrinsic value of the good and hence the reference
point, gain--loss would partially offset it. All qualitative conclusions below are
unchanged, with the joy-of-winning effect dampened; we therefore adopt the direct
specification.
\end{remark}
\subsection{Payment rules}
The payment rule determines whether the bidder's payoff conditional on winning depends
on his own action. Under a \emph{first-price} rule the winner pays his own bid, so the
payoff $v-b$ is chosen directly; under a \emph{second-price} rule he pays the rival's
bid and the price is random conditional on winning. We treat the first-price
environments in Sections~\ref{sec:pv}--\ref{sec:wallet} and the second-price
environments in Section~\ref{sec:spa}.
\section{Private-Value First-Price Auction}
\label{sec:pv}
Two symmetric bidders have i.i.d.\ private values $v\sim U[0,1]$. In a symmetric,
strictly increasing equilibrium a bid $b=\beta(P)$ wins with probability $P$. A
bidder with value $v$ who plans such a bid obtains the net payoff $\pi=v-b$ with
probability $P$ and $\pi=0$ otherwise. The reference is an independent draw from
that same two-point distribution. For $v>b$, the gain--loss term is
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}[\mu(\pi-\pi')]=-\Lambda\,P(1-P)(v-b),
\end{equation}
so that, adding the joy of winning,
\begin{equation}
U(P;v)=P(v-b)\big[1-\Lambda(1-P)\big]+wP .
\label{eq:pvobj}
\end{equation}
\begin{proposition}[Private-value first-price equilibrium, single dimension]
\label{prop:pv}
The unique symmetric, strictly increasing personal-equilibrium bidding strategy is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\ \beta^{*}(v)=\frac{w+\tfrac{1-\Lambda}{2}\,v+\tfrac{2\Lambda}{3}\,v^{2}}
{1-\Lambda+\Lambda v}\ }.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Substitute $b=\beta(P)$ in \eqref{eq:pvobj}. The first-order condition
$\partial U/\partial P=0$ at $P=v$ gives
\begin{equation}
v(1-\Lambda+\Lambda v)\,\beta'(v)+\big[1-\Lambda+2\Lambda v\big]\beta(v)
=(1-\Lambda)v+2\Lambda v^{2}+w .
\end{equation}
The left-hand side is $\frac{d}{dv}\big[\beta(v)\,v(1-\Lambda+\Lambda v)\big]$.
Integrating and imposing that $\beta(v)\,v(1-\Lambda+\Lambda v)\to0$ as $v\to0$,
\begin{equation}
\beta(v)\,v(1-\Lambda+\Lambda v)=\tfrac{1-\Lambda}{2}v^{2}+\tfrac{2\Lambda}{3}v^{3}+wv,
\end{equation}
which rearranges to the stated expression.
\end{proof}
\begin{corollary}[Overbidding and the low-value intercept]
\label{cor:pvover}
Relative to the risk-neutral benchmark $\tfrac12 v$,
\begin{equation}
\beta^{*}(v)-\tfrac12 v=\frac{w+\tfrac{\Lambda}{6}v^{2}}{1-\Lambda+\Lambda v}\ \ge\ 0 ,
\qquad
\beta^{*}(0)=\frac{w}{1-\Lambda},\qquad \beta^{*}(1)=\tfrac12+\tfrac{\Lambda}{6}+w .
\end{equation}
For $w=0$, loss aversion alone already produces overbidding at every $v>0$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Immediate from Proposition~\ref{prop:pv}.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}[Risk-neutral benchmark]
For $\Lambda=0$, $\beta^{*}(v)=\tfrac12 v+w$, recovering the standard first-price
benchmark $\tfrac12 v$ when $w=0$ and full pass-through of the joy-of-winning bonus.
For $\Lambda>0$ the denominator $1-\Lambda+\Lambda v$ is less than one for $v<1$, so
both channels are amplified relative to the linear case.
\end{remark}
\section{The First-Price Wallet Game}
\label{sec:wallet}
Two symmetric bidders receive private signals $X_i\sim U[0,1]$ i.i.d.\ and the
common value is $V=X_1+X_2$ \citep{klemperer1998,milgromWeber1982}. In a symmetric,
strictly increasing equilibrium a bid $b=\beta(P)$ wins with probability $P$, and
conditional on winning the rival's signal is $Y\sim U[0,P]$. The net payoff is
$\pi=x+Y-b$ with probability $P$ and $0$ otherwise. Compared with
Section~\ref{sec:pv}, the common value adds within-winner uncertainty. Its
gain--loss contribution is
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}[\mu(\pi-\pi')]=-\Lambda\Big[P(1-P)\Big(x+\tfrac{P}{2}-b\Big)+\tfrac{P^{3}}{6}\Big],
\end{equation}
using $\mathbb{E}[(Y-Y')^{+}]=P/6$ for $Y,Y'\sim U[0,P]$ i.i.d. Adding the joy of
winning,
\begin{equation}
U(P;x)=P\Big(x+\tfrac{P}{2}-b\Big)\big[1-\Lambda(1-P)\big]-\tfrac{\Lambda P^{3}}{6}+wP .
\label{eq:walletobj}
\end{equation}
\begin{proposition}[Wallet-game equilibrium, single dimension]
\label{prop:wallet}
The unique symmetric, strictly increasing personal-equilibrium bidding strategy is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\ \beta^{*}(x)=\frac{w+(1-\Lambda)x+\Lambda x^{2}}{1-\Lambda+\Lambda x}\ }.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Substitute $b=\beta(P)$ in \eqref{eq:walletobj}. The first-order condition at $P=x$
gives
\begin{equation}
x(1-\Lambda+\Lambda x)\,\beta'(x)+\big[1-\Lambda+2\Lambda x\big]\beta(x)
=2x(1-\Lambda)+3\Lambda x^{2}+w .
\end{equation}
The left-hand side equals
$\frac{d}{dx}\big[\beta(x)\,x(1-\Lambda+\Lambda x)\big]$. Integrating with
$\beta(x)\,x(1-\Lambda+\Lambda x)\to0$ as $x\to0$,
\begin{equation}
\beta(x)\,x(1-\Lambda+\Lambda x)=(1-\Lambda)x^{2}+\Lambda x^{3}+wx,
\end{equation}
which rearranges to the stated expression.
\end{proof}
\begin{corollary}[No loss-aversion distortion; joy of winning overbids]
\label{cor:wallet}
For all $x\in[0,1]$,
\begin{equation}
\beta^{*}(x)-x=\frac{w}{1-\Lambda+\Lambda x}\ \ge\ 0 ,
\qquad
\beta^{*}(0)=\frac{w}{1-\Lambda},\qquad \beta^{*}(1)=1+w .
\end{equation}
In particular, if $w=0$ then $\beta^{*}(x)=x$ exactly: with a single monetary
dimension, loss aversion does \emph{not} distort the wallet-game equilibrium. If
$w>0$, the bid exceeds $x$ uniformly.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Immediate from Proposition~\ref{prop:wallet}.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}[Interpretation]
The winner's curse makes the common value random conditional on winning; the
gain--loss terms in \eqref{eq:walletobj} then cancel at the margin and the
risk-neutral bid $\beta_0(x)=x$ survives. Thus loss aversion can rationalize
private-value overbidding but not, by itself, common-value overbidding. The latter
requires the joy-of-winning motive (or a non-reference mechanism such as cursedness
or bounded rationality).
\end{remark}
\section{Second-Price Auctions}
\label{sec:spa}
Under a second-price rule the winner pays the rival's bid rather than his own, so the
payment is random conditional on winning. This \emph{within-dimension price variance}
is the feature that distinguishes the second-price from the first-price format. We
analyze the two canonical environments of the preceding sections---the private-value
auction and the wallet game---under the same single-monetary-dimension preferences.
\subsection{Independent-Private-Value Second-Price Auction}
\label{sec:ipvspa}
Two symmetric bidders have i.i.d.\ private values $v\sim U[0,1]$. In a symmetric,
strictly increasing equilibrium $\beta$, a bidder who plans a bid that wins with
probability $P$ (i.e.\ $b=\beta(P)$) wins when the rival's value $Y<P$ and pays the
rival's bid $\beta(Y)$, with $Y\sim U[0,P]$. His net payoff is therefore
\begin{equation}
\pi=v-\beta(Y)\ \text{with probability }P,\quad Y\sim U[0,P];
\qquad \pi=0\ \text{with probability }1-P,
\end{equation}
and---as in Section~\ref{sec:pv}---the reference point is an independent draw from this
same distribution. Since outcomes and reference are i.i.d.,
$\mathbb{E}[\mu(\pi-\pi')]=-\Lambda\,\mathbb{E}[(\pi'-\pi)^+]$. Decomposing the positive
difference into the one-wins/one-loses and both-win events, and using
$\iint(\beta(y)-\beta(y'))^{+}dy\,dy'=\int_0^P(2y-P)\beta(y)\,dy$ for increasing
$\beta$, the interim objective is
\begin{equation}
U(P;v)=vP-\bar\beta(P)
-\Lambda\Big[(1-P)\!\int_0^P\!|v-\beta(y)|\,dy+\int_0^P(2y-P)\beta(y)\,dy\Big]+wP,
\label{eq:spaobj}
\end{equation}
where $\bar\beta(P)\equiv\int_0^P\beta(y)\,dy$.
\begin{proposition}[No loss-aversion distortion in the private-value SPA]
\label{prop:spa0}
Let $0<\Lambda<1$ and $w=0$. The unique symmetric, strictly increasing
personal-equilibrium bidding strategy is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\ \beta^{*}(v)=v\ }.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
At $w=0$ the equilibrium does not overbid, so $|v-\beta(y)|=v-\beta(y)$ for $y\le v$.
Differentiating \eqref{eq:spaobj} at $P=v$, and using
$\partial_P\int_0^P(2y-P)\beta(y)\,dy=P\beta(P)-\bar\beta(P)$,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial U}{\partial P}\Big|_{P=v}
=v-\beta(v)-\Lambda\Big[-\!\int_0^v\!\big(v-\beta(y)\big)dy+(1-v)\big(v-\beta(v)\big)
+v\beta(v)-\bar\beta(v)\Big].
\end{equation}
The term in brackets equals $v-2v^{2}+(2v-1)\beta(v)$, so the first-order condition
becomes
\begin{equation}
\big(v-\beta(v)\big)\big(1-\Lambda+2\Lambda v\big)=0 .
\end{equation}
Since $1-\Lambda+2\Lambda v\ge 1-\Lambda>0$, the unique solution is $\beta(v)=v$.
Moreover $U''(v)=-(1-\Lambda+2\Lambda v)<0$ and $\partial_P U<0$ for $P>v$, so $P=v$
is the global maximizer; truthful bidding is the unique symmetric equilibrium.
\end{proof}
\begin{proposition}[Joy of winning in the private-value SPA]
\label{prop:spa}
(i) If $\Lambda=0$, the equilibrium is $\beta^{*}(v)=v+w$ exactly. (ii) Let
$0<\Lambda<1$ and let $w>0$ be small. Then
\begin{equation}
\beta^{*}(v)=v+\frac{w}{1+\Lambda}+O(w^{2}),
\end{equation}
so bids exceed the risk-neutral benchmark at every $v$, and loss aversion
\emph{dampens} the joy-of-winning premium (the denominator $1+\Lambda$ exceeds one,
whereas in the private-value FPA the corresponding denominator $1-\Lambda+\Lambda v$
is below one).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(i) For $\Lambda=0$, \eqref{eq:spaobj} reduces to $U(P;v)=vP-\bar\beta(P)+wP$, whose
first-order condition at $P=v$ is $v-\beta(v)+w=0$, giving $\beta(v)=v+w$.

(ii) The exact first-order condition at the symmetric equilibrium, retaining the kink,
is
\begin{equation}
v-\beta(v)-\Lambda\Big[-\!\int_0^v\!|v-\beta(y)|\,dy+(1-v)\big|\beta(v)-v\big|
+v\beta(v)-\bar\beta(v)\Big]+w=0 .
\label{eq:spaexactfoc}
\end{equation}
Write $\beta(y)=y+w\phi(y)+O(w^{2})$. Since $\beta(v)-v=O(w)$, the region on which
$|v-\beta(y)|\neq v-\beta(y)$ has width $O(w)$ and contributes only $O(w^{2})$, so
$\int_0^v|v-\beta(y)|dy=\tfrac{v^{2}}{2}-w\int_0^v\phi(y)\,dy+O(w^{2})$. With
$v\beta(v)=v^{2}+wv\phi(v)+O(w^{2})$, $\bar\beta(v)=\tfrac{v^{2}}{2}+w\int_0^v\phi+O(w^{2})$
and $|\beta(v)-v|=w\phi(v)$, the bracket in \eqref{eq:spaexactfoc} equals
$w\phi(v)+O(w^{2})$. Substituting, \eqref{eq:spaexactfoc} becomes
$-w\phi(v)-\Lambda w\phi(v)+w+O(w^{2})=0$, i.e.\ $\phi(v)=1/(1+\Lambda)+O(w)$; the
second-order condition holds for small $w$ by continuity from part (i).
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}
On the interior $v\ge c$ the exact equilibrium is the constant-offset bid
$\beta^{*}(v)=v+c$, where $c$ solves $\Lambda c^{2}-(1+\Lambda)c+w=0$, i.e.
$c=\frac{(1+\Lambda)-\sqrt{(1+\Lambda)^{2}-4\Lambda w}}{2\Lambda}=\frac{w}{1+\Lambda}+O(w^{2})$.
On the boundary layer $v<c=O(w)$ every win is a net loss, so that characterization
breaks down; we therefore report the global result to leading order in $w$.
\end{remark}
\subsection{The Second-Price Wallet Game}
\label{sec:walletspsa}
Signals are $X_i\sim U[0,1]$ and the value is $V=X_1+X_2$. The risk-neutral
second-price (English) equilibrium is $\beta_0(x)=2x$: a winner at price $P$ earns
$x+\mathbb{E}[Y\mid Y<P]-P$, and bidding $2x$ equates the price to the value expected
conditional on winning.
\begin{proposition}[Wallet-game SPA under reference-dependent preferences]
\label{prop:walletspsa}
The wallet game is isomorphic to the private-value SPA. If the rival uses
$\beta(y)=2y+c$, a bidder with signal $x$ who wins at price $\beta(Y)$ earns
$x+Y-\beta(Y)=x-Y-c$ on $Y\in[0,P]$, which is exactly the private-value objective with
value $x$ and price $Y+c$. Consequently: (i) at $w=0$, $\beta^{*}(x)=2x$; (ii) at
$\Lambda=0$, $\beta^{*}(x)=2x+w$; and (iii) for small $w>0$,
$\beta^{*}(x)=2x+\frac{w}{1+\Lambda}+O(w^{2})$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Immediate from the isomorphism and Propositions~\ref{prop:spa0}--\ref{prop:spa}.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}[Interpretation]
With a single monetary dimension, loss aversion distorts neither second-price format:
the risk-neutral benchmarks $\beta_0(v)=v$ and $\beta_0(x)=2x$ survive at $w=0$. This is
the second-price counterpart of the first-price wallet-game result
(Corollary~\ref{cor:wallet}) and stands in sharp contrast to the private-value FPA,
where loss aversion alone overbids (Corollary~\ref{cor:pvover}). Overbidding in either
second-price format therefore requires the joy-of-winning motive, at a rate damped by
loss aversion.
\end{remark}
\section{Identification and Revenue}
\label{sec:id}
The single-dimension model yields the following testable implications.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Private-value overbidding under pure loss aversion.} By
Corollary~\ref{cor:pvover}, the first-price bid satisfies $\beta^*(v)>\tfrac12 v$ for
all $v>0$ even when $w=0$. The local slope of the bid function identifies $\Lambda$.
\item \textbf{Common-value overbidding requires a joy of winning.} By
Corollary~\ref{cor:wallet}, the first-price wallet-game bid equals $x$ exactly when
$w=0$ and exceeds $x$ uniformly when $w>0$. Hence any \emph{excess} common-value
overbidding (beyond the winner's curse) identifies $w>0$.
\item \textbf{No second-price distortion from loss aversion.} By
Propositions~\ref{prop:spa0} and \ref{prop:walletspsa}, when $w=0$ the second-price
private-value equilibrium is truthful, $\beta^{*}(v)=v$, and the second-price wallet
game yields $\beta^{*}(x)=2x$; loss aversion alone distorts neither. Overbidding in
either second-price format therefore again identifies $w>0$, and the \emph{contrast}
between a distorted private-value FPA and an undistorted private-value SPA isolates the
dimensionality of the gain--loss evaluation.
\item \textbf{Positive low-value bids.} A strictly positive low-value bid that is
replicated across formats requires $w>0$, which pure loss aversion cannot generate. Its
\emph{level} is format-specific---$\beta^{*}(0)=w/(1-\Lambda)$ in the FPA and, to
leading order, $w/(1+\Lambda)$ in the second-price formats---so the \emph{sign}, not
the level, is the format-invariant identifying moment.
\end{itemize}
These implications also discriminate against the two-dimensional model, whose sign
pattern is different (Appendix~\ref{app:two}).
Expected revenue in the private-value auction is
$R=2\int_0^1 x\,\beta^*(x)\,dx$, which is strictly increasing in $w$; the
wallet-game revenue is defined analogously from the first-order statistic of the two
signals. Because the joy-of-winning bonus shifts bids up while loss aversion
operates differently across formats, cross-format revenue comparisons inherit the
identification asymmetry above.
\section{Extensions}
\label{sec:ext}
\paragraph{Second-price auctions.}
These are analyzed in Section~\ref{sec:spa}. Under a single monetary dimension, loss
aversion alone leaves the risk-neutral benchmark intact---truthful bidding
$\beta_0(v)=v$ in the private-value case and $\beta_0(x)=2x$ in the wallet game---while
the joy-of-winning bonus produces overbidding at the damped rate $w/(1+\Lambda)$. For
general signal distributions the price $\beta(Y)$ enters the gain--loss integral and
the equilibrium condition becomes an equation in $\beta$ and $\bar\beta$ rather than a
closed form.
\paragraph{General signal distributions and $n$ bidders.}
For $X_i\sim F$ on $[0,1]$ and $V=\alpha(X_1+X_2)$, the problem is homogeneous in
$(\alpha,b)$ and the equilibrium scales by $\alpha$. With $n$ bidders and
$V=\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_i$, the win probability becomes $P^{\,n-1}$ and the dispersion
term scales accordingly; the same integrating-factor method applies.
\paragraph{Toward structural estimation.}
Embedding $(w,\Lambda)$ in a structural estimator that also allows for noisy bidding
\citep{goeree2002} or cursedness \citep{eysterRabin2005} would let researchers
quantify the relative contributions of joy of winning, loss aversion, and bounded
rationality. The single-dimension baseline is the appropriate starting point for
induced-value data.

\section{Two-Dimensional Comparison}
\label{app:two}
For completeness we record the multi-dimensional (good, money) results of the
private-value first-price auction and the wallet game, which require the good to be
a separate consumption dimension.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Private-value FPA:}
\begin{equation}
\beta^{*}_{2}(v)=\frac{w+\tfrac{1-\Lambda}{2}v+\tfrac{2\Lambda}{3}v^{2}}{1+\Lambda(1-v)},
\qquad
\beta^{*}_{2}(v)-\tfrac12 v=\frac{w-\Lambda v+\tfrac{7\Lambda}{6}v^{2}}{1+\Lambda(1-v)} .
\end{equation}
With $w=0$, the equilibrium \emph{underbids} for $v<6/7$ and overbids for $v>6/7$;
global overbidding requires $w\ge3\Lambda/14$.
\item \emph{Wallet game:}
\begin{equation}
\beta^{*}_{2}(x)=\frac{w+(1-\Lambda)x+\Lambda x^{2}}{1+\Lambda(1-x)},
\qquad
\beta^{*}_{2}(x)-x=\frac{w-2\Lambda x(1-x)}{1+\Lambda(1-x)} .
\end{equation}
With $w=0$, the equilibrium \emph{underbids} uniformly; global overbidding requires
$w\ge\Lambda/2$.
\item \emph{Private-value SPA:} against a truthful rival, a bidder with value $v$ who
chooses win probability $P$ obtains
$U(P;v)=vP-\tfrac{P^{2}}{2}-\Lambda P(1-P)v-\Lambda\big(\tfrac{P^{2}}{2}-\tfrac{P^{3}}{3}\big)+wP$,
so $U'(v)=-\Lambda v(2-3v)+w$. At $w=0$ the equilibrium \emph{underbids} for $v<2/3$
and overbids for $v>2/3$; global overbidding requires $w\ge\Lambda/3$.
\item \emph{Wallet SPA:} against the risk-neutral benchmark $\beta_0(x)=2x$,
$U'(x)=-\Lambda x(4-5x)+w$, so at $w=0$ the equilibrium \emph{underbids} for $x<4/5$
and overbids for $x>4/5$; global overbidding requires $w\ge4\Lambda/5$.
\end{itemize}
The denominators ($1+\Lambda(1-v)$, $1+\Lambda(1-x)$) differ from the single-dimension
denominators ($1-\Lambda+\Lambda v$, $1-\Lambda+\Lambda x$), and the sign of the
pure-loss-aversion effect reverses. In the second-price formats the two-dimensional
model likewise produces a mixed sign pattern at $w=0$ rather than the undistorted
single-dimension benchmarks $\beta^{*}(v)=v$ and $\beta^{*}(x)=2x$. The two
specifications can thus be distinguished empirically by the behavior of bids at low
valuations: single-dimension predicts overbidding near $v=0$, two-dimension predicts
underbidding.










\section{GEMINI 4 Argon - Introduction}
A robust experimental regularity is that bidders bid above the risk-neutral
Bayesian--Nash benchmark across a wide range of auction environments: first- and
second-price private-value auctions \citep{cox1982,coxSmithWalker1988,goeree2002,
crawfordIriberri2007}, and first- and second-price common-value auctions
\citep{kagelLevin1986,eysterRabin2005,crawfordIriberri2007}. Explanations include
risk aversion \citep{cox1982,coxSmithWalker1988}, anticipated regret
\citep{engelbrechtWiggans1989,filizOzbay2007}, quantal response and level-$k$
reasoning \citep{goeree2002,crawfordIriberri2007}, cursedness \citep{eysterRabin2005},
and a non-monetary utility of winning \citep{delgado2008}.

Expectations-based reference dependence \citep{koszegiRabin2006,koszegiRabin2007},
as applied to sealed-bid auctions by \citet{langeRatan2010} and \citet{banerji2014},
is a leading candidate. Its canonical auction formulation, however, is
\emph{multi-dimensional}: the good and money are treated as separate consumption
dimensions, which is what generates the ``attachment'' to the object. This is a
substantial assumption in induced-value laboratory auctions, where the object being
sold is a fictitious token whose entire worth is a cash redemption value paid by the
experimenter. There, the good and money dimensions are economically identical, and
it is natural for subjects to evaluate gain--loss over their net monetary earnings.

This paper takes the induced-value setting seriously and restricts attention to a
\emph{single monetary dimension}, with preferences defined over the net monetary
payoff and a choice-acclimating reference point \citep[CPE;][]{koszegiRabin2007}.
In a single dimension, K\H{o}szegi--Rabin preferences generate \emph{first-order
risk aversion} \citep{koszegiRabin2007}: the kink in the gain--loss function makes
agents averse to small mean-preserving spreads in a way that ordinary
expected-utility risk aversion does not. This channel has sharply different
implications from the two-dimensional model, so the choice of dimensionality is not
innocuous.

To this baseline we add a single reference-independent parameter: a direct utility
bonus $w\ge0$ obtained upon winning, the ``joy of winning'' \citep{delgado2008}.
Because the bonus is direct rather than a change in the good's monetary redemption
value, it is not itself subject to monetary gain--loss comparison and is well
defined whether or not the good is a separate dimension. The model nests pure loss
aversion at $w=0$ and admits closed-form equilibria across the canonical auction
formats.

Our results are as follows. In the private-value first-price auction, loss aversion
alone ($w=0$) generates overbidding for \emph{every} valuation $v\in(0,1]$; a
positive joy-of-winning bonus ($w>0$) amplifies overbidding on the interior and
yields a strictly positive bid at a zero valuation, $\beta^*(0)=w/(1+\Lambda)$,
which pure loss aversion cannot. In the first-price wallet game of
\citet{klemperer1998}, loss aversion alone has \emph{no} effect: at $w=0$ the
equilibrium coincides with the risk-neutral benchmark $\beta_0(x)=x$. Any strictly
positive joy-of-winning bonus, however, produces uniform overbidding. Likewise, in
both private-value and wallet-game second-price auctions, pure loss aversion leaves
the risk-neutral equilibrium intact ($\beta^*(v)=v$ and $\beta^*(x)=2x$), whereas
$w>0$ generates overbidding of $w/(1+\Lambda)$ to leading order. This delivers a
clean, testable asymmetry: \emph{private-value first-price} overbidding is
consistent with pure loss aversion, while \emph{common-value} overbidding (beyond
the winner's curse) and \emph{second-price} overbidding require a joy-of-winning
motive. Together with the format-invariant low-valuation intercept $w/(1+\Lambda)$,
this provides a parsimonious set of moments to separate the two motives.

Section~\ref{sec:framework} presents the framework and discusses dimensionality.
Section~\ref{sec:pv} and Section~\ref{sec:wallet} analyze the private-value auction
and the wallet game in the first-price format; Section~\ref{sec:spa} analyzes the
two second-price environments. Section~\ref{sec:id} discusses identification and
revenue. Section~\ref{sec:ext} sketches extensions. Appendix~\ref{app:two} states
the two-dimensional results for comparison.

\section{Framework}
\label{sec:framework}

\subsection{Preferences over a single monetary dimension}
Consumption is the net monetary payoff $\pi\in\mathbb{R}$. Following
\citet{koszegiRabin2006,koszegiRabin2007}, utility relative to a deterministic
reference payoff $r$ is
\begin{equation}
u(\pi\mid r)=\pi+\mu(\pi-r),
\end{equation}
where $\mu$ is the piecewise-linear gain--loss function
\begin{equation}
\mu(x)=\begin{cases}\eta\,x & x\ge0,\\ \eta\lambda\,x & x<0,\end{cases}
\qquad \lambda>1,\ \eta>0 .
\end{equation}
We normalize $\eta=1$ (or absorb $\eta$ into the composite parameter) and write
$\Lambda\equiv\eta(\lambda-1)\in[0,1)$ for the degree of loss aversion. Following
\citet{koszegiRabin2007} and \citet{banerji2014}, we adopt the
\emph{Choice-Acclimating Personal Equilibrium} (CPE) solution concept: when a
bidder commits to a bid $b$, the reference distribution adjusts to the lottery
$F(b)$ of net monetary payoffs induced by that bid before uncertainty is resolved.
Expected utility is therefore the expectation of $u(\pi\mid\pi')$ over independent
draws $\pi,\pi'\sim F(b)$:
\begin{equation}
U(F(b)\mid F(b)) = \mathbb{E}[\pi] + \mathbb{E}[\mu(\pi-\pi')]
= \mathbb{E}[\pi] - \Lambda\,\mathbb{E}\big[(\pi-\pi')^{+}\big],
\end{equation}
where $z^{+}\equiv\max\{z,0\}$. Because $\mu$ is kinked at zero, CPE preferences
over a single dimension exhibit first-order risk aversion \citep{koszegiRabin2007},
penalizing dispersion in the net payoff distribution via the Gini mean difference
$\mathbb{E}[(\pi-\pi')^{+}]$.

\subsection{Dimensionality and induced values}
\label{sec:dim}
The multi-dimensional auction formulation of \citet{langeRatan2010} separates the
good ($k=1$) and money ($k=2$):
$u(c\mid r)=\sum_{k=1}^2[m_k(c_k)+\mu(m_k(c_k)-m_k(r_k))]$. There, winning the
auction yields a gain on the good dimension and a loss on the money dimension, so
the two never offset. That separation requires $m_1$ to be a consumption domain
psychologically distinct from money (such as a physical object or commodity). In an
induced-value laboratory auction, the item is a token automatically redeemed for cash
at the end of the round, so the subject simply receives a net cash transfer $v-b$.
We therefore use the single-dimension formulation above throughout the main text and
provide the exact two-dimensional CPE counterparts in Appendix~\ref{app:two}. Which
dimensionality describes laboratory behavior is ultimately an empirical question;
the two models make opposite predictions at low valuations (see
Section~\ref{sec:id}).

\subsection{Joy of winning}
A bidder who wins the auction receives a direct non-monetary utility bonus $w\ge0$,
modeled additively as $w\cdot P$, where $P$ is the probability of winning. Being a
direct psychological reward upon winning rather than a monetary cash flow, this term
is reference-independent and valid under either dimensionality.

\begin{remark}[Alternative specification]
If $w$ were also evaluated relative to an expectations-based reference point on a
separate ``ego/winning'' dimension, a bidder winning with probability $P$ would
experience gain--loss $-\Lambda_w P(1-P)w$ in addition to $wP$. For small $w$, this
merely rescales the effective bonus without altering our qualitative conclusions; we
therefore adopt the parsimonious direct specification.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Payment rules}
The payment rule determines whether the bidder's monetary payoff conditional on
winning is deterministic or random. Under a \emph{first-price} rule the winner pays
his own bid, so the private-value winning payoff $v-b$ is deterministic; under a
\emph{second-price} rule he pays the rival's bid, introducing price variance
conditional on winning. We analyze the first-price environments in
Sections~\ref{sec:pv}--\ref{sec:wallet} and the second-price environments in
Section~\ref{sec:spa}.

\section{Private-Value First-Price Auction}
\label{sec:pv}
Two symmetric bidders have i.i.d.\ private values $v\sim U[0,1]$. Suppose the rival
uses a strictly increasing, differentiable strategy $\beta:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}_+$. A
bidder with value $v$ who chooses a bid $b=\beta(P)$ wins with probability $P\in[0,1]$
and obtains the net monetary payoff $\pi=v-b$ with probability $P$ and $\pi=0$ with
probability $1-P$. Comparing against an independent draw $\pi'$ from the same
two-point distribution, the expected gain--loss term is
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}[\mu(\pi-\pi')]=-\Lambda\,P(1-P)\,|v-b|.
\end{equation}
Thus the interim CPE objective is
\begin{equation}
U(P;v)=P(v-b)-\Lambda\,P(1-P)\,|v-b|+wP .
\label{eq:pvobj_gen}
\end{equation}
Notice that the sign of $v-b$ matters. When $w=0$, bidders never bid above their
valuation ($b\le v$), so $|v-b|=v-b$ everywhere:
\begin{equation}
U(P;v)=P(v-b)\big[1-\Lambda(1-P)\big]+wP \qquad (\text{valid for } b\le v).
\label{eq:pvobj}
\end{equation}
When $w>0$, a bidder with a very low valuation ($v$ near $0$) will bid above $v$
($b>v$) to capture the joy-of-winning bonus $w$. On that low-valuation boundary
layer $[0,\bar v)$, winning entails a net monetary loss ($v-b<0$), so $|v-b|=b-v$
and the objective becomes
\begin{equation}
U(P;v)=P(v-b)\big[1+\Lambda(1-P)\big]+wP \qquad (\text{valid for } b>v).
\label{eq:pvobj_loss}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Private-value first-price equilibrium, single dimension]
\label{prop:pv}
Let $0\le\Lambda<1$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \textbf{Pure loss aversion ($w=0$):} The unique symmetric, strictly
increasing CPE bidding strategy on $[0,1]$ is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\ \beta^{*}(v)=\frac{\tfrac{1-\Lambda}{2}\,v+\tfrac{2\Lambda}{3}\,v^{2}}
{1-\Lambda+\Lambda v}\ }.
\label{eq:pv_w0}
\end{equation}
\item[(ii)] \textbf{With joy of winning ($w>0$):} Let $w>0$ be sufficiently small
that $\bar v\in(0,1)$, where $\bar v$ is the unique root in $(0,1)$ of
$w=\tfrac{1+\Lambda}{2}\bar v-\tfrac{\Lambda}{3}\bar v^{2}$. Then on the boundary
layer $v\in[0,\bar v]$ (where $\beta^{*}(v)\ge v$),
\begin{equation}
\beta^{*}(v)=\frac{w+\tfrac{1+\Lambda}{2}\,v-\tfrac{2\Lambda}{3}\,v^{2}}
{1+\Lambda-\Lambda v},
\qquad \text{with }\ \beta^{*}(0)=\frac{w}{1+\Lambda},
\label{eq:pv_bl}
\end{equation}
and on the interior $v\in[\bar v,1]$ (where $\beta^{*}(v)\le v$),
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\ \beta^{*}(v)=\frac{w_{\mathrm{eff}}+\tfrac{1-\Lambda}{2}\,v+\tfrac{2\Lambda}{3}\,v^{2}}
{1-\Lambda+\Lambda v}\ },
\label{eq:pv_int}
\end{equation}
where $w_{\mathrm{eff}}\equiv w+\Lambda\bar v^{2}\big(1-\tfrac{7}{6}\bar v\big)=w+O(w^{2})$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $v$ in the region where $\beta(v)\le v$, substitute $b=\beta(P)$ into
\eqref{eq:pvobj}. The first-order condition $\partial U/\partial P=0$ at $P=v$ gives
\begin{equation}
v(1-\Lambda+\Lambda v)\,\beta'(v)+\big[1-\Lambda+2\Lambda v\big]\beta(v)
=(1-\Lambda)v+2\Lambda v^{2}+w .
\label{eq:pv_ode_pos}
\end{equation}
The left-hand side is $\frac{d}{dv}\big[\beta(v)\,v(1-\Lambda+\Lambda v)\big]$.
When $w=0$, integrating from $0$ to $v$ with $\beta(0)=0$ yields \eqref{eq:pv_w0},
which satisfies $\beta^*(v)\le v$ and $\beta^{*\prime}(v)>0$ for all $v\in[0,1]$.

When $w>0$, $\beta^*(0)>0$, so $\beta^*(v)>v$ on an interval $[0,\bar v)$. On
$[0,\bar v]$, \eqref{eq:pvobj_loss} applies, which replaces $\Lambda$ by $-\Lambda$
in \eqref{eq:pv_ode_pos}:
\begin{equation}
\frac{d}{dv}\Big[\beta(v)\,v(1+\Lambda-\Lambda v)\Big]
=(1+\Lambda)v-2\Lambda v^{2}+w .
\end{equation}
Integrating from $0$ to $v$ and imposing $\beta(v)v(1+\Lambda-\Lambda v)\to 0$ as
$v\to 0$ gives \eqref{eq:pv_bl}. Setting $\beta^*(\bar v)=\bar v$ pins down $\bar v$
via $w+\tfrac{1+\Lambda}{2}\bar v-\tfrac{2\Lambda}{3}\bar v^{2}=\bar v(1+\Lambda-\Lambda\bar v)$,
or $w=\tfrac{1+\Lambda}{2}\bar v-\tfrac{\Lambda}{3}\bar v^{2}$ (so
$\bar v=\frac{2w}{1+\Lambda}+O(w^2)$). For $v\ge\bar v$, integrating
\eqref{eq:pv_ode_pos} from $\bar v$ to $v$ with continuity $\beta^*(\bar v)=\bar v$
yields \eqref{eq:pv_int}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Overbidding and the low-value intercept]
\label{cor:pvover}
Relative to the risk-neutral benchmark $\tfrac12 v$, for all $v\in[\bar v,1]$,
\begin{equation}
\beta^{*}(v)-\tfrac12 v=\frac{w_{\mathrm{eff}}+\tfrac{\Lambda}{6}v^{2}}{1-\Lambda+\Lambda v}\ >\ 0 ,
\qquad
\beta^{*}(0)=\frac{w}{1+\Lambda},\qquad
\beta^{*}(1)=\tfrac12+\tfrac{\Lambda}{6}+w_{\mathrm{eff}} .
\end{equation}
For $w=0$ ($\bar v=0$, $w_{\mathrm{eff}}=0$), loss aversion alone produces strict
overbidding at every $v\in(0,1]$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Immediate from Proposition~\ref{prop:pv}. On $[0,\bar v]$,
$\beta^*(v)\ge v>\tfrac12 v$, so overbidding holds globally on $[0,1]$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Intuition for the boundary layer and interior]
For $\Lambda=0$, $\beta^{*}(v)=\tfrac12 v+w$, recovering the standard first-price
benchmark $\tfrac12 v$ when $w=0$ and full pass-through of the joy-of-winning bonus.
For $\Lambda>0$ and $v>\bar v$, the denominator $1-\Lambda+\Lambda v$ is less than
one, so first-order risk aversion amplifies overbidding: a higher bid reduces the
payoff gap $v-b$ between winning and losing, shrinking the gain--loss penalty. At
$v=0$, however, winning requires paying $b>0$ for a worthless token, which is a
pure monetary loss relative to losing ($0$); loss aversion therefore dampens the
zero-valuation intercept to $\beta^*(0)=w/(1+\Lambda)$.
\end{remark}

\section{The First-Price Wallet Game}
\label{sec:wallet}
Two symmetric bidders receive private signals $X_i\sim U[0,1]$ i.i.d.\ and the
common value is $V=X_1+X_2$ \citep{klemperer1998,milgromWeber1982}. In a symmetric,
strictly increasing equilibrium a bid $b=\beta(P)$ wins with probability $P$, and
conditional on winning the rival's signal is $Y\sim U[0,P]$. The net monetary payoff
is $\pi=x+Y-b$ with probability $P$ and $0$ with probability $1-P$.

Whenever $b\le x$, the realized winning payoff $\pi=x+Y-b$ is non-negative for all
$Y\in[0,P]$. Decomposing $\mathbb{E}[(\pi-\pi')^{+}]$ into the win-vs.-lose event
and the both-win event (and using $\mathbb{E}[(Y-Y')^{+}]=P/6$ for $Y,Y'\sim U[0,P]$
i.i.d.), the gain--loss contribution is
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}[\mu(\pi-\pi')]=-\Lambda\Big[P(1-P)\Big(x+\tfrac{P}{2}-b\Big)+\tfrac{P^{3}}{6}\Big].
\end{equation}
Adding the joy of winning, the interim objective for $b\le x$ is
\begin{equation}
U(P;x)=P\Big(x+\tfrac{P}{2}-b\Big)\big[1-\Lambda(1-P)\big]-\tfrac{\Lambda P^{3}}{6}+wP .
\label{eq:walletobj}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Wallet-game equilibrium, single dimension]
\label{prop:wallet}
Let $0\le\Lambda<1$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \textbf{Pure loss aversion ($w=0$):} The unique symmetric, strictly
increasing CPE bidding strategy on $[0,1]$ is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\ \beta^{*}(x)=x\ }.
\label{eq:wallet_w0}
\end{equation}
\item[(ii)] \textbf{With joy of winning ($w>0$):} At $\Lambda=0$,
$\beta^*(x)=x+w$ for all $x\in[0,1]$. For $0<\Lambda<1$ and small $w>0$,
\begin{equation}
\beta^{*}(0)=\frac{w}{1+\Lambda},
\end{equation}
and for all $x\in[\bar x,1]$ (where $\bar x=O(w)$ is the boundary layer on which
$x-\beta^*(x)<0$), the equilibrium satisfies
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\ \beta^{*}(x)=x+\frac{w}{1-\Lambda+\Lambda x}+O(w^{2})\ }.
\label{eq:wallet_wpos}
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) Set $w=0$ and substitute $b=\beta(P)$ into \eqref{eq:walletobj}. The first-order
condition $\partial U/\partial P=0$ at $P=x$ gives
\begin{equation}
x(1-\Lambda+\Lambda x)\,\beta'(x)+\big[1-\Lambda+2\Lambda x\big]\beta(x)
=2x(1-\Lambda)+3\Lambda x^{2} .
\end{equation}
The left-hand side equals
$\frac{d}{dx}\big[\beta(x)\,x(1-\Lambda+\Lambda x)\big]$, and the right-hand side is
$\frac{d}{dx}\big[x^{2}(1-\Lambda+\Lambda x)\big]$. Integrating from $0$ with
$\beta(0)=0$ yields $\beta^*(x)=x$. At $b=x$, the winning payoff is $x+Y-x=Y\ge 0$
for all $Y\in[0,x]$, confirming that \eqref{eq:walletobj} holds globally on $[0,1]$.

(ii) When $w>0$, $\beta^*(x)>x$. At $x=0$, any win yields $Y-b<0$ near $P\to 0$, so
by the same boundary-layer argument as in Proposition~\ref{prop:pv},
$\beta^*(0)=w/(1+\Lambda)$. For $x$ bounded away from $0$ and $w$ small,
$\beta(x)-x=O(w)$, so the subinterval of $Y\in[0,P]$ on which $x+Y-\beta(P)<0$ has
length $\beta(P)-x=O(w)$. On that subinterval the integrand $|x+Y-\beta(P)|$ differs
from $x+Y-\beta(P)$ by $2(\beta(P)-x-Y)=O(w)$, so the integral over $[0,\beta(P)-x]$
contributes $\Lambda(1-P)(\beta(P)-x)^2=O(w^2)$ to $U(P;x)$ and $O(w^2)$ to the
first-order condition. Thus, up to $O(w^2)$, the first-order condition on $[\bar x,1]$
is
\begin{equation}
\frac{d}{dx}\Big[\beta(x)\,x(1-\Lambda+\Lambda x)\Big]
=2x(1-\Lambda)+3\Lambda x^{2}+w + O(w^{2}),
\end{equation}
which integrates to \eqref{eq:wallet_wpos}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[No loss-aversion distortion; joy of winning overbids]
\label{cor:wallet}
If $w=0$, then $\beta^{*}(x)=x$ for all $x\in[0,1]$: with a single monetary
dimension, loss aversion does \emph{not} distort the first-price wallet-game
equilibrium. If $w>0$, the equilibrium exhibits strict overbidding
($\beta^{*}(x)>x$) for all $x\in[0,1]$, with
\begin{equation}
\beta^{*}(x)-x=\frac{w}{1-\Lambda+\Lambda x}+O(w^{2}) \quad (x\ge\bar x),
\qquad
\beta^{*}(0)=\frac{w}{1+\Lambda},\qquad
\beta^{*}(1)=1+w+O(w^{2}) .
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Immediate from Proposition~\ref{prop:wallet}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Interpretation]
In the wallet game, conditional on winning at the risk-neutral benchmark $b=x$, the
bidder's net payoff is $\pi=x+Y-x=Y\sim U[0,x]$. Raising the bid slightly both
increases the probability of winning (which reduces win--lose dispersion when $x$ is
large) and increases the within-winner common-value dispersion $\Lambda P^3/6$ (since
a higher $P$ admits higher rival signals $Y$). Under a single monetary dimension,
these marginal gain--loss effects cancel identically against the marginal cost of
raising the bid, leaving $\beta_0(x)=x$ undistorted at $w=0$. Thus, pure loss
aversion can rationalize private-value FPA overbidding but not common-value
overbidding.
\end{remark}

\section{Second-Price Auctions}
\label{sec:spa}
Under a second-price rule the winner pays the rival's bid rather than his own, so the
payment is random conditional on winning. We analyze the private-value auction and
the wallet game under the same single-monetary-dimension CPE preferences.

\subsection{Independent-Private-Value Second-Price Auction}
\label{sec:ipvspa}
Two symmetric bidders have i.i.d.\ private values $v\sim U[0,1]$. In a symmetric,
strictly increasing equilibrium $\beta$, a bidder who chooses a bid $b=\beta(P)$ wins
with probability $P$ (when the rival's value $Y<P$) and pays the rival's bid
$\beta(Y)$, with $Y\sim U[0,P]$. His net monetary payoff is therefore
\begin{equation}
\pi=v-\beta(Y)\ \text{with probability }P\ (Y\sim U[0,P]),
\qquad \pi=0\ \text{with probability }1-P.
\end{equation}
Comparing against an independent draw $\pi'$ from this same distribution,
$\mathbb{E}[\mu(\pi-\pi')]=-\Lambda\,\mathbb{E}[(\pi'-\pi)^+]$. Decomposing the
expected positive difference into the one-wins/one-loses event (probability
$2P(1-P)$) and the both-win event (probability $P^2$), and using
$\int_0^P\int_0^P(\beta(y)-\beta(y'))^{+}dy\,dy'=\int_0^P(2y-P)\beta(y)\,dy$ for any
increasing function $\beta$, the exact interim CPE objective is
\begin{equation}
U(P;v)=vP-\bar\beta(P)
-\Lambda\Big[(1-P)\!\int_0^P\!|v-\beta(y)|\,dy+\int_0^P(2y-P)\beta(y)\,dy\Big]+wP,
\label{eq:spaobj}
\end{equation}
where $\bar\beta(P)\equiv\int_0^P\beta(y)\,dy$.

\begin{proposition}[No loss-aversion distortion in the private-value SPA]
\label{prop:spa0}
Let $0\le\Lambda<1$ and $w=0$. The unique symmetric, strictly increasing CPE bidding
strategy is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\ \beta^{*}(v)=v\ }.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
In a candidate equilibrium where $\beta(v)\le v$, $|v-\beta(y)|=v-\beta(y)$ for all
$y\le v$. Differentiating \eqref{eq:spaobj} with respect to $P$ at $P=v$, and using
$\partial_P\int_0^P(2y-P)\beta(y)\,dy=P\beta(P)-\bar\beta(P)$, gives
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial U}{\partial P}\Big|_{P=v}
=v-\beta(v)-\Lambda\Big[-\!\int_0^v\!\big(v-\beta(y)\big)dy+(1-v)\big(v-\beta(v)\big)
+v\beta(v)-\bar\beta(v)\Big].
\end{equation}
The two integral terms $\int_0^v\beta(y)\,dy-\bar\beta(v)$ cancel identically,
leaving $(v-\beta(v))(1-2v)$ inside the bracket. The first-order condition becomes
\begin{equation}
\big(v-\beta(v)\big)\big(1-\Lambda+2\Lambda v\big)=0 .
\end{equation}
Since $1-\Lambda+2\Lambda v\ge 1-\Lambda>0$ for all $v\in[0,1]$, the unique solution
is $\beta^*(v)=v$. Moreover, against a truthful rival $\beta(y)=y$, the global
derivative of \eqref{eq:spaobj} for any $P\in[0,1]$ is
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial U(P;v)}{\partial P}
=\begin{cases}
(v-P)\big(1-\Lambda(1-2P)\big) > 0 & \text{if } P < v, \\
(v-P)\big(1+\Lambda(1-2v)\big) < 0 & \text{if } P > v,
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
since $1\pm\Lambda(1-2z)\ge 1-\Lambda>0$ for all $z\in[0,1]$. Thus $P=v$ is the
unique global maximizer for every $v\in[0,1]$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Joy of winning in the private-value SPA]
\label{prop:spa}
(i) If $\Lambda=0$, the unique symmetric equilibrium is $\beta^{*}(v)=v+w$ for all
$v\in[0,1]$. (ii) Let $0<\Lambda<1$ and let $w>0$ be small. Then for all $v\in[0,1]$,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\ \beta^{*}(v)=v+\frac{w}{1+\Lambda}+O(w^{2})\ },
\end{equation}
so bids exceed the risk-neutral benchmark at every $v$, and loss aversion
\emph{dampens} the joy-of-winning premium.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) For $\Lambda=0$, \eqref{eq:spaobj} reduces to $U(P;v)=vP-\bar\beta(P)+wP$, whose
first-order condition at $P=v$ is $v-\beta(v)+w=0$, giving $\beta^*(v)=v+w$.

(ii) Retaining the absolute value in \eqref{eq:spaobj}, the exact first-order
condition at $P=v$ is
\begin{equation}
v-\beta(v)-\Lambda\Big[-\!\int_0^v\!|v-\beta(y)|\,dy+(1-v)\big|\beta(v)-v\big|
+v\beta(v)-\bar\beta(v)\Big]+w=0 .
\label{eq:spaexactfoc}
\end{equation}
Write $\beta(y)=y+w\phi(y)+O(w^{2})$ with $\phi(y)>0$. For $v\ge w\phi(v)$, the
subinterval of $[0,v]$ on which $v-\beta(y)<0$ has width $O(w)$ and integrand
$O(w)$, contributing $O(w^{2})$ to the integral; for $v<w\phi(v)$ (where $v=O(w)$),
the entire integral over $[0,v]$ is $O(w^2)$ and $(1-v)|\beta(v)-v|=w\phi(v)+O(w^2)$.
In both cases,
\begin{equation}
-\!\int_0^v\!|v-\beta(y)|\,dy+v\beta(v)-\bar\beta(v)=O(w^{2}),
\qquad (1-v)\big|\beta(v)-v\big|=w(1-v)\phi(v)+w v\phi(v)+O(w^{2})=w\phi(v)+O(w^{2}).
\end{equation}
Wait: let us check the $v\phi(v)$ terms carefully! For $v\ge c$,
$-\int_0^v(v-y-w\phi(y))\,dy + v(v+w\phi(v)) - \int_0^v(y+w\phi(y))\,dy = -\frac{v^2}{2} + w\int_0^v\phi + v^2 + wv\phi(v) - \frac{v^2}{2} - w\int_0^v\phi = wv\phi(v)$.
Adding $(1-v)w\phi(v)$ gives $wv\phi(v) + (1-v)w\phi(v) = w\phi(v)$! And at $v=0$,
the bracket is simply $|\beta(0)-0|=w\phi(0)$. Thus, for all $v\in[0,1]$, the bracket
in \eqref{eq:spaexactfoc} equals $w\phi(v)+O(w^{2})$. Substituting this into
\eqref{eq:spaexactfoc} gives
\begin{equation}
-w\phi(v)-\Lambda w\phi(v)+w+O(w^{2})=0 \implies \phi(v)=\frac{1}{1+\Lambda}+O(w).
\end{equation}
Global optimality holds for small $w$ by concavity of $U(P;v)$ from Proposition~\ref{prop:spa0}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Exact constant-offset solution on the interior]
On the interior $v\ge c$, substituting the constant-offset ansatz $\beta^{*}(v)=v+c$
into \eqref{eq:spaexactfoc} yields $\int_0^v|v-y-c|\,dy=\tfrac{v^2}{2}-vc+c^2$ and
$v\beta(v)-\bar\beta(v)=\tfrac{v^2}{2}$, so the bracket in \eqref{eq:spaexactfoc}
equals $c-c^2$ identically in $v$. Thus $\beta^{*}(v)=v+c$ is the \emph{exact}
solution on $[c,1]$, where $c$ solves $\Lambda c^{2}-(1+\Lambda)c+w=0$, i.e.,
\begin{equation}
c=\frac{(1+\Lambda)-\sqrt{(1+\Lambda)^{2}-4\Lambda w}}{2\Lambda}
=\frac{w}{1+\Lambda}+\frac{\Lambda w^{2}}{(1+\Lambda)^{3}}+O(w^{3}).
\end{equation}
On the boundary layer $v\in[0,c)$, $|v-y-c|=y+c-v$ on all of $[0,v]$, so the slope
varies by $O(w^2)$, while at $v=0$ the exact condition $-\beta(0)(1+\Lambda)+w=0$
gives $\beta^*(0)=w/(1+\Lambda)$ exactly.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The Second-Price Wallet Game}
\label{sec:walletspsa}
Signals are $X_i\sim U[0,1]$ i.i.d.\ and the common value is $V=X_1+X_2$. The
risk-neutral second-price (and two-bidder English) equilibrium is $\beta_0(x)=2x$: a
bidder who wins at marginal signal $P=x$ pays $\beta_0(x)=2x$, which equals the
upper bound of the support of $x+Y$ conditional on winning.

\begin{proposition}[Wallet-game SPA under reference-dependent preferences]
\label{prop:walletspsa}
Let $0\le\Lambda<1$. The second-price wallet game is strategically isomorphic to the
private-value SPA:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] At $w=0$, the unique symmetric, strictly increasing CPE strategy is
$\boxed{\beta^{*}(x)=2x}$.
\item[(ii)] At $\Lambda=0$, $\beta^{*}(x)=2x+w$ for all $x\in[0,1]$.
\item[(iii)] For $0<\Lambda<1$ and small $w>0$,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\ \beta^{*}(x)=2x+\frac{w}{1+\Lambda}+O(w^{2})\ },
\qquad \text{with }\ \beta^{*}(0)=\frac{w}{1+\Lambda}.
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Suppose the rival uses a strategy of the form $\beta(y)=y+\tilde\beta(y)$, where
$\tilde\beta$ is strictly increasing. A bidder with signal $x$ who chooses win
probability $P$ wins when $Y<P$ and earns the net monetary payoff
\begin{equation}
\pi = x + Y - \beta(Y) = x - \tilde\beta(Y), \qquad Y\sim U[0,P],
\end{equation}
and $\pi=0$ with probability $1-P$. Because the distribution of $\pi=x-\tilde\beta(Y)$
is identical to the net payoff distribution of a bidder with private value $v=x$ facing
a rival with bid function $\tilde\beta(Y)$ in the private-value SPA, the interim CPE
objective $U(P;x)$ is identical to \eqref{eq:spaobj} with $\tilde\beta$ in place of
$\beta$. By Propositions~\ref{prop:spa0} and \ref{prop:spa}, the unique equilibrium
$\tilde\beta^*$ satisfies $\tilde\beta^*(x)=x$ at $w=0$, $\tilde\beta^*(x)=x+w$ at
$\Lambda=0$, and $\tilde\beta^*(x)=x+\frac{w}{1+\Lambda}+O(w^2)$ for small $w>0$.
Since $\beta^*(x)=x+\tilde\beta^*(x)$, the results follow immediately.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Interpretation]
With a single monetary dimension, pure loss aversion ($w=0$) distorts neither
second-price format: the risk-neutral benchmarks $\beta_0(v)=v$ and $\beta_0(x)=2x$
survive intact. At the margin $P=v$ (where $v-\beta_0(v)=0$) or $P=x$ (where
$x+x-\beta_0(x)=0$), winning against the highest-type rival yields zero net surplus,
which equals the losing payoff $0$. Consequently, a marginal change in $P$ at the
truth-telling point causes no first-order jump in payoff dispersion, and the kink in
$\mu$ does not shift the optimal bid when $w=0$. When $w>0$, however, the marginal
winning payoff at $P=v$ is negative ($-c<0$), so raising $P$ creates a first-order
monetary loss relative to losing, which loss aversion penalizes at rate $\Lambda$,
damping the overbidding offset to $w/(1+\Lambda)$.
\end{remark}

The single-dimension framework yields four sharp, testable implications:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Private-value FPA overbidding under pure loss aversion.} By
Corollary~\ref{cor:pvover}, the first-price bid satisfies $\beta^*(v)>\tfrac12 v$ for
all $v>0$ even when $w=0$. The curvature and slope of the bid function on the
interior identify $\Lambda$.
\item \textbf{Common-value FPA overbidding requires a joy of winning.} By
Corollary~\ref{cor:wallet}, the first-price wallet-game bid equals $x$ when $w=0$ and
exceeds $x$ uniformly when $w>0$. Hence any common-value overbidding beyond the
winner's curse identifies $w>0$ (or a non-reference behavioral channel such as
cursedness).
\item \textbf{No second-price distortion from pure loss aversion.} By
Propositions~\ref{prop:spa0} and \ref{prop:walletspsa}, when $w=0$ the second-price
private-value equilibrium is truthful, $\beta^{*}(v)=v$, and the second-price wallet
game yields $\beta^{*}(x)=2x$. Overbidding in either second-price format therefore
identifies $w>0$, while the \emph{contrast} between a distorted PV-FPA and an
undistorted PV-SPA isolates first-order risk aversion over a single monetary
dimension.
\item \textbf{Format-invariant zero-valuation intercept.} Across all four formats
(PV-FPA, CV-FPA, PV-SPA, and CV-SPA), a bidder with a zero valuation or signal bids
\begin{equation}
\beta^{*}(0)=\frac{w}{1+\Lambda},
\end{equation}
because winning with a zero signal entails a sure monetary loss equal to the price
paid, so loss aversion dampens the joy-of-winning motive by the same factor
$1/(1+\Lambda)$ in every format. A strictly positive intercept $\beta^*(0)>0$
identifies $w>0$, while the interior amplification in FPA ($\propto 1/(1-\Lambda+\Lambda v)$)
versus damping in SPA ($\propto 1/(1+\Lambda)$) separately identifies $(w,\Lambda)$.
\end{enumerate}

These predictions also cleanly discriminate between the single-dimension and
two-dimensional specifications. Under the two-dimensional model with $w=0$
(Appendix~\ref{app:two}), bidders \emph{underbid} at low valuations in all four
formats because fear of paying money dominates attachment to the good when the
probability of winning is below $1/2$. Observing overbidding across low and moderate
valuations in induced-value experiments therefore favors the single-dimension
specification.

Finally, expected revenue in the two-bidder first-price auction is
$R_{\mathrm{FPA}}=2\int_0^1 x\,\beta_{\mathrm{FPA}}^*(x)\,dx$, while in the
second-price auction it is
$R_{\mathrm{SPA}}=2\int_0^1 (1-x)\,\beta_{\mathrm{SPA}}^*(x)\,dx$. Because loss
aversion raises bids in PV-FPA and amplifies the pass-through of $w$ on the interior
 while damping $w$ in PV-SPA, $R_{\mathrm{FPA}} > R_{\mathrm{SPA}}$ holds strictly in
private-value auctions whenever $\Lambda>0$.

\section{Extensions}
\label{sec:ext}
\paragraph{General signal distributions and $n$ bidders.}
For $V=\alpha(X_1+X_2)$ with $\alpha>0$, the monetary payoff is homogeneous of
degree one in $(\alpha,b,w)$, so all equilibrium bids scale by $\alpha$ (with $w$
replaced by $w/\alpha$). With $n\ge 2$ symmetric bidders and $v_i\sim U[0,1]$ in the
PV-FPA, the win probability is $P=v^{n-1}$ in equilibrium, so the single-dimension
objective $P(v-b)[1-\Lambda(1-P)]+wP$ yields a linear ODE with integrating factor
$v^{n-1}[1-\Lambda(1-v^{n-1})]$, preserving a closed-form rational solution.

\paragraph{Personal Equilibrium (PE/PPE) versus CPE.}
Throughout we have used Choice-Acclimating Personal Equilibrium (CPE), where the
reference distribution adjusts to the chosen bid. If instead expectations are fixed
at the moment the bid is submitted (PE/PPE, as in \citealt{langeRatan2010}), a local
deviation in $b$ does not alter the reference lottery. Even in that setting, the
distinction between a single monetary dimension and two separate dimensions remains
fundamental: collapsing the good and money dimensions removes the cross-dimensional
attachment loss $-\Lambda v$ upon losing, shifting predicted bids upward relative to
the two-dimensional PPE benchmark.

\paragraph{Toward structural estimation.}
Embedding $(w,\Lambda)$ in a structural econometric model that also allows for noisy
best responses \citep{goeree2002} or cursedness \citep{eysterRabin2005} enables
researchers to decompose observed overbidding into joy of winning, reference-dependent
risk attitudes, and bounded rationality. The single-dimension model developed here
provides the theoretically appropriate baseline for induced-value laboratory data.

\appendix
\section{Two-Dimensional Comparison}
\label{app:two}
For completeness we record the multi-dimensional (good $k=1$, money $k=2$) CPE
results when the good and money are evaluated in separate gain--loss accounts.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Private-value FPA:} The CPE objective is
$U_2(P;v)=P(v-b)-\Lambda P(1-P)(v+b)+wP$. The first-order condition at $P=v$ is
$\frac{d}{dv}[\beta(v)v(1+\Lambda(1-v))]=(1-\Lambda)v+2\Lambda v^2+w$, which
integrates to
\begin{equation}
\beta^{*}_{2}(v)=\frac{w+\tfrac{1-\Lambda}{2}v+\tfrac{2\Lambda}{3}v^{2}}{1+\Lambda(1-v)},
\qquad
\beta^{*}_{2}(v)-\tfrac12 v=\frac{w-\tfrac{\Lambda}{2}v+\tfrac{7\Lambda}{6}v^{2}}{1+\Lambda(1-v)} .
\end{equation}
With $w=0$, the equilibrium \emph{underbids} for $v<6/7$ and overbids for $v>6/7$;
global overbidding ($\beta^*_2(v)\ge \tfrac12 v$ for all $v\in[0,1]$) requires
$w\ge 3\Lambda/56$ (the maximum of $\tfrac{\Lambda}{2}v-\tfrac{7\Lambda}{6}v^2$ on
$[0,1]$, attained at $v=3/14$). Notice also that $\beta^*_2(0)=w/(1+\Lambda)$.

\item \textbf{First-price wallet game:} The CPE objective is
$U_2(P;x)=P(x+\tfrac{P}{2}-b)-\Lambda P(1-P)b-\Lambda[P(1-P)(x+\tfrac{P}{2})+\tfrac{P^3}{6}]+wP$.
The first-order condition at $P=x$ integrates to
\begin{equation}
\beta^{*}_{2}(x)=\frac{w+(1-\Lambda)x+\Lambda x^{2}}{1+\Lambda(1-x)},
\qquad
\beta^{*}_{2}(x)-x=\frac{w-2\Lambda x(1-x)}{1+\Lambda(1-x)} .
\end{equation}
With $w=0$, the equilibrium \emph{underbids} uniformly on $(0,1)$; global overbidding
requires $w\ge\Lambda/2$ (attained at $x=1/2$), and $\beta^*_2(0)=w/(1+\Lambda)$.

\item \textbf{Private-value SPA:} For a general increasing strategy $\beta(y)$, the
two-dimensional CPE objective is
\begin{equation}
U_2(P;v)=vP-\bar\beta(P)-\Lambda P(1-P)v
-\Lambda\Big[(1-P)\bar\beta(P)+\int_0^P(2y-P)\beta(y)\,dy\Big]+wP.
\end{equation}
Against a naive truthful rival ($\beta_0(y)=y$), the marginal incentive to deviate at
$P=v$ is $\partial_P U_2(v;v)=-\Lambda v(2-3v)+w$, which at $w=0$ points downward
(underbidding) for $v<2/3$ and upward (overbidding) for $v>2/3$ (requiring
$w\ge\Lambda/3$ to be non-negative everywhere). More generally, setting
$\partial_P U_2(P;v)|_{P=v}=0$ yields the exact symmetric equilibrium integral
equation
\begin{equation}
(1+\Lambda)\beta^*_2(v)-2\Lambda\int_0^v\beta^*_2(y)\,dy = v\big[1-\Lambda(1-2v)\big]+w,
\end{equation}
which solves in closed form via the integrating factor $e^{-\frac{2\Lambda}{1+\Lambda}v}$
with intercept $\beta^*_2(0)=w/(1+\Lambda)$.

\item \textbf{Second-price wallet game:} Against the risk-neutral benchmark
$\beta_0(x)=2x$, the good dimension has dispersion $P(1-P)(x+P/2)+P^3/6$ and the
money dimension has dispersion $P(1-P)P+P^3/3$, giving marginal deviation incentive
$\partial_P U_2(x;x)=-\Lambda x(4-5x)+w$ at $P=x$. At $w=0$, the bidder has a strict
incentive to underbid for $x<4/5$ and overbid for $x>4/5$; a globally positive
deviation incentive requires $w\ge 4\Lambda/5$.
\end{itemize}

In sum, the two-dimensional denominators ($1+\Lambda(1-v)$ and $1+\Lambda(1-x)$)
differ from the interior single-dimension denominators ($1-\Lambda+\Lambda v$ and
$1-\Lambda+\Lambda x$), and the sign of the pure-loss-aversion effect at low and
moderate signals reverses. The two specifications can therefore be distinguished
empirically by bidding behavior at low and intermediate valuations.
